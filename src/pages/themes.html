Themes

Three themes, each with a daylight half and a dark one. The same project, the same file, the same window size — so what differs between these pictures is only the colour.

⌘, → Appearance, which is the first page and holds the editor's colours and the terminal's together — one thing to look at, so one place to decide it. It takes effect on the windows that are already open: the palette is swapped underneath them rather than applied to the next one.

Two questions rather than one list. Theme is which palette — Abydos, Blue or Dracula — and Light or dark is how light it should be, with System as the third answer to the second question rather than an entry in the first. That is what a single list could not say: somebody who wants the warm palette wants it in the morning too, and somebody who follows the system wants the theme they chose to follow along rather than be swapped for another one. Three by three, and the six that are not a deferral are below.

Every picture on this page was taken by make theme-shots, which runs the app once per palette with --theme and captures the window. The colours listed under each are read out of the app's own Theme.swift, so what is named here is what is drawn.

Abydos

Settings: Abydos + Dark Terminal scheme: abydos The default for the pictures on this site

The app's own palette: a sun on the horizon, which is what the icon is. Amber earns its place three times over — it is CRT phosphor, it is desert, and it is a warm ground in a category that is wall to wall blue. The surfaces are nearly black with warmth in them rather than brown: a brown editor is tiring within the hour, and what should be warm is the light falling on it, not the paper.

Abydos Light

Settings: Abydos + Light Terminal scheme: abydos, at daylight weights What Abydos + System resolves to in the day

Not the dark one inverted, and this is the theme where that matters most. Amber on near-black works because the amber is the light in the room; the same amber on paper is a highlighter pen. So the warmth moves into the surfaces instead — unbleached paper rather than white — and the ink is a dark brown that reads as black until it is next to one. The accent stays amber and is taken down to where it can be read on paper, which is roughly the colour of that pigment in shadow. Comments are the one thing that moves rather than darkens: a grey-brown at this weight disappears, so they keep a little green and sit clearly behind the code.

Dusk

Settings: Blue + Dark What Blue + System resolves to after dark

The dark theme, for anyone who wants the familiar arrangement: quiet chrome, one warm colour for keywords, green for the text you wrote, blue for the things that are called. The greys carry a little more blue than most, and the syntax hues are pulled apart so keyword, type and call read as three colours rather than as three shades of one.

Daylight

Settings: Blue + Light What Blue + System resolves to in the day

Not the dark palette inverted. A light theme wants more contrast in the text and less in the surfaces, or every panel edge shouts — so the greys sit close together and the colours do the separating. The editor is the one white surface on the screen, which is what makes it the thing you look at.

Dracula

Settings: Dracula + Dark Terminal scheme: Dracula's own sixteen, unchanged The one palette here that is not ours

People arrive with this one rather than discover it here — from a terminal, an editor, a whole desktop — and that is the whole point of offering it. So the values are upstream's rather than adjusted to taste, and the six accents keep the roles Dracula assigns them: pink for keywords, green for functions, purple for constants and numbers, cyan for types, orange for parameters, yellow for strings. Somebody who picks it should see the same file in the same colours as in whatever they came from. Only the roles this app has and Dracula does not — a git status, a fold placeholder — are placed, each given the accent that already means it elsewhere.

Alucard

Settings: Dracula + Light What Dracula + System resolves to in the day Upstream's own daylight counterpart

Every theme here comes in both lightnesses, and Dracula upstream is dark only — but it publishes a daylight counterpart, Alucard, for exactly the reason that inverting the dark one gives neon on white. So the light half is not invented here: role for role with the dark one, in Alucard's darkened hues on its warm off-white. Alucard publishes eight hues rather than a full table, so the terminal's bright half is each of them lifted — which is what the dark scheme's bright half already is.

System

System is not a palette but a deferral, and it belongs to the second question rather than the first: it follows the Mac's own light and dark setting without touching which theme you chose. Abydos plus System is amber after dark and warm paper by day; Blue gives dusk and daylight; Dracula gives Dracula and Alucard. It changes while the app is running, so a machine that switches at sunset switches the editor with it.

It used to be one list, and one list could not say that. Abydos and Abydos Light sat in it as two entries, System sat in it as a fifth — and the thing most people actually want, the warm palette following the light, was the one combination there was no way to ask for. Five entries saying four things, and still not the one that mattered.
